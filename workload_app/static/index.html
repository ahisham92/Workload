What these measures mean

Timesheets

Everyone's exports, month by month. They are the only thing the app needs: every other tab is worked out from them.

Every night, on its own

Each PC on the team asks BISpark for its own export once a day, as soon as it is on the Dar network, signed in with its Windows login, and leaves it in the team's shared folder. Every 6 hours, if anything in the folder is new, your PC sends it here. Nothing is installed: the kits use only what Windows already has.

Import exports

Choose everyone's export together, or just the ones that changed. Each row goes to whoever booked it, so there is nobody to pick. Somebody new joins the team, and a job number with no project becomes one — named by its number, with its phases as deliverables — for you to confirm on Projects.

Projects

Every job number the timesheets turn up, its budget, how far along it is and what it has earned.

Reports

Every figure is worked out once from the unit's own data and shared between the views.

Task management

The work in front of the team, who it belongs to, and whether it fits in the days available. This tab stands on its own — nothing here touches the timesheet, a project's progress or anyone's CPI.

Team

Who this unit's engineers are. Everything follows from here — the split on each deliverable, capacity and the scorecard.

Reference tables

The project types and the rules of credit every deliverable is measured against.

Planner

Everybody's day, filled in from the timesheets, the submissions plan and what comes in. The only thing to type is a request.

Check-ins

Who has free hours, who has been overloaded and needs a lighter week, who is fresh and can take more, and what to ask each person. Nothing to fill in: it all comes from the timesheets, the tasks and the calendar.
